AI Isn't Taking Over, It's Augmenting Decision-Making

Nov 15, 2024

4 min read

/

Gavin Laugenie

It's a Saturday afternoon, and I'm at a kid's birthday party. The obligatory small talk is in full swing, and after discussing whose kid belongs to whom and comparing notes on cupcake flavors, I hear the inevitable question, "So, what do you do?"

I hesitate because I know how this usually goes. When I say "AI," it's almost like I can hear people's eyes roll. They, of course, picture an army of T-1000s or Mr. Smith from “The Matrix.” But today, I decided to lean into it. AI, after all, is now my thing — and I happen to love what I do.

"I work in AI," I say, bracing for the reaction.
I meet new and interesting people all the time, and bracing for their reaction to me working in AI these days is just part of the routine. So, strap in. This is the first in a series of conversations where, in different "real world" settings, I'll be tackling some of the biggest misconceptions people have about AI and showing why it's actually not as intimidating as it seems. Anyway, back to the kids' party…

The Job Replacement Myth

Sure enough, one of the parents gives me a sympathetic look and says, "AI, huh? Doesn't that mean you're working to replace us all with robots?" She laughs, but there's a hint of genuine concern. It's a misconception I run into all the time.

First off, I explain that I work for
Dataiku
. And here’s where I see a shift in the room. AI is one part of the story, but at Dataiku, we go beyond just “AI.” Our platform — the Universal AI Platform — is designed around collaboration, governance, security, and no-/low-code capabilities to ensure everyone, from data scientists to business teams, can get involved. The raised eyebrows turn into intrigued looks as I go on to explain that Dataiku is as much about empowering teams as it is about the technology.

But I want to make it clear that AI isn't here to take over jobs, it's here to make our work more accessible and more impactful. In my field, particularly at Dataiku, AI empowers people to make better, faster decisions. Far from being a job thief, AI is more like the ultra-smart assistant behind the scenes that cuts out the grunt work, letting people focus on the parts of their jobs that require a human touch.
I tell them AI doesn’t just exist in isolation on our platform. It’s a tool we believe should be accessible to anyone in an organization, technical or not. Dataiku makes it possible for cross-functional teams to work together seamlessly, enhancing decision-making while maintaining security and governance at every step. And with our no-/low-code options, anyone in the company can experiment with data and insights without needing to be a coding expert.

Imagine a data scientist who spends hours crunching numbers to make sense of customer behavior. With an AI-powered platform like Dataiku, that same data scientist can use automated tools to find patterns in minutes. The AI handles the heavy lifting, freeing up time for strategy, creativity, and decision-making.

AI as an "Insight Partner"

I’ll give an example to help everyone understand how it works. Take a marketing team that wants to predict which customers might stop using a service. Traditionally, that would mean days, maybe even weeks, of analyzing spreadsheets and searching for patterns. However, with AI, teams can find these insights with just a few clicks.

The key is that AI delivers insights rather than answers. It can show patterns or suggest next steps, but people still make the final call. The platform supports you as you sift through complex data, but ultimately, the decisions — and the creative thinking behind them — are all human.

The Helpful Assistant, Not the Boss

I can see that some of the parents are still on the fence, so I try a different approach: "Think of it this way," I say. "Imagine you have an assistant who's amazing with numbers. They can sort through data and bring you only the pieces that matter. That's AI; it's here to help you be more efficient, not to take over your job."

At Dataiku, one of our goals is to make AI a tool not just for data scientists but for anyone who needs it, even if they don't have a coding background. That includes sales teams, customer service,
finance
, you name it. Our platform allows non-technical teams to easily experiment with AI-driven tools to answer questions or forecast outcomes. For instance, a sales team could use AI to forecast quarterly performance trends without digging through historical data.

This accessibility means that anyone in the organization can make faster, smarter decisions grounded in data without needing to be a tech wizard. So, whether you're in
marketing
looking for churn indicators,
finance
forecasting revenue, or operations optimizing logistics, Dataiku enables anyone to leverage AI and data-driven insights quickly, easily, and collaboratively.

More Focus on What People Do Best

As the conversation continues, I can see that the tone has shifted. The idea of AI as an "insight partner" is much less intimidating. I explain that AI removes repetitive, time-consuming tasks, the kind that can make work feel like a chore, leaving people with the parts of their jobs they truly enjoy.

With Dataiku, teams have more time to spend on creative strategies, customer relationships, and problem-solving. That's what makes my job so rewarding: seeing people focus on the meaningful parts of their work while AI handles the rest.

Demystifying AI — One Birthday Party at a Time

As the conversation wraps up, I can see the parents are looking at AI differently. Instead of a “job stealer,” they see it as an empowering tool. And for me, that's a win. So, the next time someone asks what I do, I might skip the cowering and proudly explain that AI isn't about taking over; it's about teaming up.

After all, when used right, AI is here to make our work lives easier, smarter, and more fulfilling. And who wouldn’t want that? But if you think AI is only for the “techies” in the room, think again. In my next post in the series, I’ll share how AI is breaking out of the IT department and becoming a tool for everyone, no matter what the vertical.
