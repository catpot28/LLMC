Frequently asked questions
What is conversational AI?
Conversational AI is a category of artificial intelligence that enables software to understand, process, and respond to language in a natural human-like manner. Unlike traditional chatbots that follow rigid scripts, conversational AI uses natural language processing (NLP), large language models (LLMs), and machine learning to interpret what someone actually means, maintain context across a conversation, and generate relevant responses in real time.
In practice, conversational AI powers everything from voice agents that handle customer support calls to chat assistants that help users navigate a website, make purchases, or book appointments. Without human intervention.
How does conversational AI work?
Conversational AI works through a pipeline of interconnected technologies. When a user speaks or types, the system first converts that input into something it can process. For voice, this means automatic speech recognition (ASR) transcribes audio into text. Natural language understanding (NLU) then interprets the meaning, intent, and context behind the words. A language model generates an appropriate response, and for voice applications, text-to-speech (TTS) converts that response back into natural-sounding audio.
What separates modern conversational AI from older systems is that each of these steps is powered by machine learning, meaning the system improves over time. Advanced platforms like ElevenLabs co-locate all of these components, transcription, understanding, generation, and synthesis, to minimize latency and deliver responses that feel like real conversation rather than a back-and-forth with a machine.
What is the difference between conversational AI and a chatbot?
A chatbot is a specific application. Conversational AI is the underlying technology. Traditional chatbots are rule-based. They follow decision trees and match keywords to pre-written responses. They work fine for simple FAQs but break down the moment a user asks something unexpected or phrases a question in a way the system wasn't programmed for.
Conversational AI, by contrast, understands intent and context. It can handle ambiguity, follow multi-turn conversations, remember what was said earlier, and generate original responses rather than selecting from a script. A traditional chatbot is like a phone tree in text form. A conversational AI is like talking to someone who actually understands you.
What is the difference between conversational AI and generative AI?
Generative AI is a broad category of AI that "generates" content such as text, images, code, audio, video, based on patterns learned from training data. Conversational AI is a specific application of generative AI focused on real-time dialogue.
Think of it this way. Generative AI can write an essay, create an image, or compose music. Conversational AI uses many of the same underlying technologies but applies them specifically to back-and-forth human interaction, where understanding context, managing turn-taking, maintaining memory, and responding in real time all matter.
Most modern conversational AI platforms, including ElevenLabs, orchestrate many AI models to create conversational experiences. For example, we use Scribe to transcribe your speech, an LLM to understand the context of a conversation and our TTS model to generate the response. This pipeline creates real-time natural conversations and dialogues.
What are common use cases for conversational AI?
Conversational AI is used across virtually every industry where businesses communicate with customers or employees at scale. The most common use cases include customer support automation (handling inquiries, troubleshooting, and ticket resolution 24/7), outbound sales and lead qualification (AI agents that make calls, qualify prospects, and book meetings), appointment scheduling (healthcare, hospitality, financial services), order management and transactional support (checking balances, processing returns, updating accounts), employee-facing applications (IT helpdesk, HR onboarding, internal knowledge retrieval), and accessibility (voice-based interfaces for users who can't navigate traditional digital channels).
