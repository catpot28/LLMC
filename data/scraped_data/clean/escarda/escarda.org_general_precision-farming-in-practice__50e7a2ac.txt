Precision Farming in Practice: Targeted Weed Control for Modern Agriculture

Definition:

Precision farming refers to the management of agricultural operations at plant or site level, applying inputs only where and when they are needed.

Precision farming has become a practical necessity. Rising labor costs, herbicide restrictions, and shrinking margins force farms to act with greater accuracy- especially in weed control, one of the most cost-intensive field operations.

What Precision Farming Means in Practice:

Precision farming focuses on targeted action instead of uniform treatment. Rather than treating entire fields, actions are applied selectively to individual plants or defined field zones.

Key characteristics of precision farming include:

Plant-level or site-specific decision-making

Reduced input use through targeted application

Repeatable and measurable results

Compatibility with existing farm workflows

Precision Weed Control as a Core Discipline:

Weed control is a defining use case for precision farming. Laser-based weed control systems identify individual weeds and remove them precisely, without disturbing crops or soil. This enables site-specific farming with maximum accuracy and minimal collateral impact.

Why Early-Stage Precision Matters:

Acting at early growth stages significantly improves efficiency. Removing weeds when they are only a few millimeters in size reduces energy input, increases effectiveness, and ensures consistent field performance.

Precision Farming vs. Smart Farming:

Precision farming defines where and how actions are applied. Smart farming defines how decisions are generated using digital technologies. Precision farming sets the strategy; smart farming provides the tools.

At Escarda, precision farming is applied through laser-based weed control that combines plant-level accuracy with industrial reliability, safety, and scalability – designed for real European field conditions.

Precision farming is no longer an innovation trend. It is an operational requirement for farms seeking resilience, regulatory security, and long-term economic viability.
