What We Look For
We seek smart, driven problem-solvers from a wide range of disciplines—whether you come from law, medicine, engineering, or another field entirely. What matters most is your ability to think critically, tackle complex challenges, and create meaningful impact. We’re committed to attracting individuals from all backgrounds, because with diverse perspectives, we can deliver distinctive solutions for our clients and create a welcoming, inclusive culture.
And we are particularly excited about people who resonate with
our values
.

Values and Integrity
Our values
are at the heart of everything we do, guiding how we work and who we hire. They shape how we collaborate, mentor, and encourage every voice to be heard—hallmarks of our non-hierarchical, diverse meritocracy.

During your interview, you may hear stories about these values in action, like our culture of apprenticeship and support or the expectation to engage and dissent. We encourage you to explore these values and discuss them in your conversations.

Our commitment to integrity also informs how we adopt new technologies like AI, and how we apply it in our work and hiring. We expect candidates to use AI responsibly, ensuring a fair process where we can understand your skills and experiences.

AI in the Hiring Process
We’re proud to be at
the forefront of AI
—shaping, building, and using cutting-edge tools every day. We work alongside global leaders on high-stakes AI topics and equip our people with training and access to advanced AI solutions—like our proprietary platform Lilli, ChatGPT Enterprise, and other emerging tools so they can strengthen their AI skills, work smarter, and make a bigger impact. We welcome those who share our curiosity about AI and its potential.

We know many candidates use AI to prepare, whether that’s polishing a resume or practicing interview questions—just as people have long sought feedback from mentors, friends, or family. When used responsibly, we see AI as a valuable tool that supports preparation.

Misuse, however, compromises fairness. Using AI to misrepresent yourself or to generate real-time responses during an interview goes against our values, creates an uneven playing field, and prevents us from understanding who you truly are. We want to get to know you, not your AI tool.

Client-facing roles
Application process
As part of our application process, we may ask you to complete a test or participate in a game, depending on the client-facing role you’re applying for. These assessments are designed to focus on skills and knowledge related to the job, such as problem-solving, coding abilities, and more.
Solve: A Fun Problem-Solving Challenge
One of the exciting steps in our process for most consulting roles is
Solve
, a gamified assessment
that tests your natural problem-solving abilities. There’s no need to prepare—just approach it with curiosity and creativity.
Interview process
For most client-facing roles, you will participate in a personal experience interview, followed by a problem-solving interview. Depending on the role, you may also have expertise interviews. Your recruiter will confirm your interview details in advance. Interviewing helps us learn about you as a person and a potential colleague, and it helps you learn more about McKinsey, our people and what you could do here.
Personal Experience Interview (PEI)
We like to hear about your experiences—both accomplishments and challenges—to discover skills that will help you thrive at McKinsey. Come prepared to discuss some important experiences in a detailed way, focusing on your specific role and describing the key actions that were critical to success.
We suggest coming prepared with
two personal examples
that demonstrate how you’ve made an impact in each of the four areas below. Remember to share the details of the challenges and the specific actions you took to drive meaningful change. We’re excited to get to know you better through this process and learn about what makes you unique.

Problem Solving Interview
We believe that the best way to demonstrate your problem-solving skills is by discussing a typical McKinsey client scenario. During the Problem-Solving Interview—sometimes referred to as a case interview—we’ll present a business case to evaluate your analytical thinking and approach to solving complex problems.
To help you prepare,
we recommend reviewing the sample cases provided below
. These cases contain logic, thought processes, and suggested answers. Practicing them will help you become familiar with the types of questions we ask and boost your confidence when navigating the problem-solving conversation.
Beautify
Evaluating whether a global beauty products company should be training in-store beauty consultants in the effective use of virtual channels to connect with customers.
Diconsa
Assessing the use of the Diconsa network in Mexico to provide basic financial services offerings to the rural poor for the Gates Foundation.
Electro-Light
Designing a product launch of a flavored sports drink with reduced sugar content to help replace electrolytes for a major beverage company.
Talbot Trucks
Determining whether to invest in the production and sale of electric trucks for a leading European truck manufacturer.
Expertise Interview
Depending on the role for which you’re interviewing, you may be invited to participate in an
expertise interview
. This is an opportunity for us to gain a deeper understanding of how your experience and skill set will enable you to excel in the role. If you’re applying for a
technical role
, you’ll undergo an assessment tailored to evaluate your specific technical skills. This
may include coding challenges
, problem-solving tasks, or other role-specific exercises designed to assess your proficiency in the technical areas most relevant to the position.

Internal roles
Interviews
Your interview experience will differ depending on the role for which you apply. Several include an online assessment, writing test, or a group exercise. All candidates have at least one individual interview, and the number of colleagues you meet will vary.

But regardless of the path or office location you pursue, the process is consistent, fair, and offers you the opportunity to showcase your skills, learn more about the role, and meet our colleagues so you can get to know McKinsey.

So how should you prepare for your interviews? Relax and be yourself. Bring questions for us. Come prepared to share your experiences, knowledge, skills, and accomplishments.

Experience Day
Some roles, such as executive assistant, business presentation specialist, and certain visual graphics roles, include participation in an Experience Day. This takes place either virtually or at one of our
McKinsey Global Services (MGS) offices
. Experience Days may involve a role simulation to assess your skills, a group exercise where you’ll collaborate with others to problem-solve, and interviews with McKinsey colleagues to ask questions and learn more about our firm and culture. The day is designed to give you a realistic preview of the role, its responsibilities, and the necessary skills, ensuring that both you and the interviewers can feel confident about whether the role is a good fit.
Virtual Interviews
Our goal is for any virtual interviews to closely match the experience of meeting in person. Here are some tips candidates find helpful.

Choose a quiet, private space
to ensure you can concentrate. Headphones can help minimize background noise.

Gather the necessities.
Keep your laptop charger, pen and paper and water within reach.

Disable previously installed AI software
(such as note-taking tools or virtual assistants), as these may unintentionally record or assist with the interview.

Keep your camera on.
This will help us connect with you, and you with us.

Check your own video feed:
do a test run to ensure your camera is positioned correctly and any virtual backgrounds or blurring features are disabled.

Speak up.
If a question isn’t clear, ask your interviewer to repeat it. This is your interview; we want you to be comfortable so you can perform at your best.

Just as with in‑person interviews, we’re committed to a fair, objective process and expect our candidates to adhere to our
Assessment Integrity Expectations
.
