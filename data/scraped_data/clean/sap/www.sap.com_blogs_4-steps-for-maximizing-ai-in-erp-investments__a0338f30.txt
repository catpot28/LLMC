Artificial intelligence-driven ERP solutions are no longer just a promising technology on the horizon—they are here, reshaping enterprise management as we know it. Advanced AI tools can now automate complex processes, predict trends, and make recommendations for smarter decision-making. The technology is already helping enterprises boost their performance and profit margins. This makes AI adoption more vital than ever for companies wanting to protect their competitive edge.

However, choosing the wrong AI capabilities can prove costly, wasting precious resources and stunting future growth. With a well-planned, strategic approach, businesses can secure a strong return on their AI in ERP investments.

At SAP, we’ve helped customers across industries navigate their AI transformations. Along the way, we’ve found that AI implementation doesn't need to be a dramatic overhaul. Modest, practical changes can still yield rich rewards now, all while laying a solid foundation for future innovation. Consider incorporating these realistic best practices to jump-start your own successful AI adoption journey.

1. Start your AI journey with a cloud-first strategy

AI and cloud connectivity go hand in hand, as
cloud-based systems
offer the only way for most businesses to access data-driven intelligence. AI models require intense computing power far beyond average on-premises IT systems. AI solutions also need real-time, accurate data to understand your unique business context. Enterprise management cloud applications help resolve these roadblocks by offering an agile, cost-effective platform for deploying new AI models and managing data from multiple sources.

With this in mind, build your cloud infrastructure first before diving into any major AI investments. SAP Business Suite streamlines this process by combining AI and data capabilities with cloud enterprise management applications like SAP Cloud ERP—all supported by SAP Business Technology Platform. Cloud connectivity helps harmonize data across SAP and third-party systems, building a solid foundation to fuel AI. By uniting applications across different business areas, the cloud also enables AI to perform complex, cross-functional roles.

2. Prioritize AI practicality over hype

AI promises sweeping business transformations, changing the way we work. But businesses shouldn’t just adopt AI for AI’s sake. Enterprises should first determine their operational gaps and build their AI strategy from there. To identify AI solutions that target your strategic goals:

Define specific business cases for AI integration

Outline your specific business cases for adopting AI, using data to establish clear baselines and performance targets. Business cases pinpoint exactly what problems AI can help you solve—like reducing costs, improving customer service, or increasing efficiency. With precise numbers, you can also track metrics over time to determine your ROI. This approach guides you toward AI capabilities that actually align with your business goals.

Make end users part of your AI adoption journey

AI solutions ultimately need to fulfill end users’ real-world requirements, so actively engage them in the AI implementation process. They can help identify current pain points in your enterprise resource planning processes that could benefit from AI integration.

3. Start small and build your AI on a foundation of trust

More people are turning to AI in their daily personal lives. But for high-stakes, real-world business scenarios, many naturally feel cautious about the technology’s reliability and accuracy. To earn your team’s confidence, start small. Introduce a few
relevant business AI use cases
and collect feedback from end users to assess their practical value. From there, you can better identify which areas require additional AI capabilities. This approach also encourages user buy-in by showing how AI can actively improve their work experience.

4. Make autonomous ERP your ultimate goal

AI will reshape your ERP experiences as end users become more familiar with the technology. To realize AI’s full potential, however, businesses should consider autonomous ERP systems their ultimate goal. The end user will still stay in the driver’s seat. They set the task parameters and select the final course of action. But AI will automate the painstaking groundwork that doesn’t require human decision-making, reacting on users’ behalf to automate tasks. Over time, AI can learn from users’ past decisions to manage even more roles. This frees teams to concentrate on what they do best—creative problem-solving and innovation.

Combining business AI and ERP software doesn't have to be an overwhelming task. By starting small and focusing on practical, relevant solutions, you can build trust, foster adoption, and pave the way for future, more ambitious AI-driven initiatives. With this approach, AI can become a powerful ally supporting your resilience and growth.
