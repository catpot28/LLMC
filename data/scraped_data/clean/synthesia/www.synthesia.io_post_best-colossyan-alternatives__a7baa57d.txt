The 5 Best Colossyan Alternatives In 2026 (Tried & Tested)
By
Kyle Odefey
Video Producer
at Synthesia
Create AI videos with 240+ avatars in 160+ languages.
Get started for FREE
What are the best alternatives to Colossyan?
‍
Synthesia
:
Best for interactive training and enablement
‍
HeyGen
:
Expressive avatars with fast rendering
Camtasia
:
Built for screen-first editing workflows with basic avatars
Vyond
: Animation-first video platform with basic avatars
‍
Elai
:
L&D focused avatar tool with super-fast video rendering
Colossyan is an AI video platform that helps L&D (learning and development) teams create
video-led training
.
It offers a slide-based video editor and a number of L&D-focused features including SCORM exports, LMS integrations, completion analytics, branching scenarios, and interactive quizzes.
However, many users find that the avatar quality isn't quite good enough. Common complaints include that the avatar gestures feel rigid and pre-programmed, that the avatars have weak emotional delivery, and that the platform has limited B-roll options.
In my own testing of Colossyan I also had a number of issues with unreliable video rendering.
As a result of these drawbacks, it might be worth exploring alternatives.
Here are the best Colossyan alternatives; I've tried to flag each alternative's specific selling point and which use case they are best suited to.
How I tested these Colossyan alternatives
I tested each of these
AI avatar platforms
using exactly the same script in both English and Spanish in order to ensure a fair and consistent comparison of avatar quality.
I evaluated each platform both on its own and specifically against Colossyan. Colossyan is an L&D-focused tool, so I paid special attention to the L&D use case.
I tried to focus on the criteria that are most important to people when they are working with AI avatars in an L&D context, which in my opinion are:
Avatar realism
Lip sync accuracy
Interactivity
SCORM compatibility and analytics
Localization quality
Enterprise readiness
Collaboration features
Overall stability
How nice the platform is to use
On average I spent about 1 hour testing each tool.
1. Synthesia
URL:
https://www.synthesia.io/
Quick summary
Avatar realism:
High realism
Lip-sync accuracy:
High accuracy
Voice quality:
High
Best use case:
Training, enablement, and enterprise comms
Biggest limitation:
Slower rendering
My experience
Pros
As you can see in my test video above, Synthesia's AI avatars have a very high level of realism. The combination of natural facial expressions, subtle head and hand movements, and even interesting micro-expressions all result in a convincing avatar performance.
I think the lip sync looks very accurate and seemed to be stable across multiple video generations and even when I tested out sentences that were on the longer side.
The Synthesia avatar library has more than 240 stock avatars to choose from, many of which you can customize (i.e. change their clothes or the background).
In terms of custom avatars, the platform offers the ability to generate synthetic avatars based on a prompt, or you can make an avatar that looks like you or someone else (with their permission via a recorded consent video) using an image, a webcam recording, or by booking a studio session.
There is an option to do a one-click translation of any video made within Synthesia. For translation of existing videos (made outside of Synthesia) the platform also offers
AI dubbing.
Synthesia offers two main routes for creating a video. You can upload a document, paste a URL, type a script, or input a prompt to create a video via the Assistant feature, which allows you to get a quick start and then iterate on your video via a chatbot.
The other option is to use Synthesia's video editor, which feels a lot like PowerPoint since it uses a similar slide-based layout.
While the editor is script-based, there's also a timeline view. Regardless of which route you choose, you'll probably end up using one of the many video templates which help give your video structure and a nice design that you can easily adjust to match any brand kit.
It's worth also mentioning Synthesia's AI Playground, which gives you free access to
AI video generation
models like Veo 3 and Sora 2. You can then download these assets or use them as B-roll in your avatar-driven videos.
One really cool feature is the ability to 'direct' your avatar to take actions in scenes that you prompt, which really helps to widen the range of videos that you can make with AI avatars.
It moves the whole format beyond 'talking head' videos and towards something more dynamic. The platform also lets you make really beautiful motion graphics that help make your videos look really professional.
Synthesia supports interactivity with options to add knowledge checks, clickable buttons, and branching scenarios to your video. It also lets you export videos as SCORM packages so you can upload them to an LMS and track learner completion.
Cons
One downside with Synthesia is that it only supports Chrome and Microsoft Edge - so there is no Safari support. I use Safari as my default browser on my Mac, so this was a bit of a pain.
Lastly, I'd flag Synthesia's video rendering times as being slightly longer than most of the other options on this list. During my test it took me 6 minutes to render the English version and close to 10 mins to render the Spanish one.
The rendering times have improved a lot since my test though.
How does Synthesia compare to Colossyan?
Synthesia's avatars are of a much higher quality than Colossyan's. They are much more realistic, have much better lip-sync, and express a much wider range of emotions.
I think this is particularly important in an L&D context where you want the learner to be focused on what the video is trying to communicate, rather than distracting them with avatars that at best are a bit strange to look at, and at worst sometimes unsettling.
Synthesia also offers the ability to direct your avatars to take actions (e.g. pointing, interacting with objects, and more) and I think this, along with Synthesia's motion graphics capabilities, help you to
create training videos
that are much more engaging than you can with Colossyan.
Colossyan is probably the cheaper of the two platforms overall in terms of enterprise pricing, so I could see some L&D teams choosing Colossyan if they are very cost-conscious and are looking for a 'good enough' solution.
Another feature worth calling out in Colossyan's favor is the platform's Conversation Mode, which lets you add up to four avatars in one single scene. Synthesia supports multiple avatars too, but I do like how easy Colossyan's tool makes it to create back-and-forth conversations.
I think the level of interactivity you can add to your videos is relatively similar across both platforms, and they both offer an integrated screen recording tool and SCORM exports.
2. HeyGen
URL:
https://www.heygen.com/
Quick summary
Avatar realism:
High realism
Lip-sync accuracy:
High accuracy
Voice quality:
High
Best use case:
Marketing videos
Biggest limitation:
Not specialized for L&D
My experience
Pros
I think HeyGen is in the top three of avatar platforms (along with Synthesia and Creatify) in terms of avatar realism. HeyGen's avatars are very expressive, with natural facial movements and body language.
I think the realism is also significantly enhanced by little touches like posture shifts, blinking, and other micro-gestures. I'd also call out the handling of hand and hair movement as being particularly good. These are often areas where AI avatars have issues.
In general, I found HeyGen's lip sync to be highly accurate. In my test video above you can see how the avatar's mouth movements are precisely aligned with the timing of the speech.
HeyGen's
avatar creation process
is fast and easy. I was able to generate a full avatar in around 15 seconds, which is on the lower end compared to the tools I've tested here, and the platform was also pretty quick to render my video.
I found the platform itself to be easy to use with a clean and easy-to-navigate UI, even with some of the advanced features the platform offers.
Cons
HeyGen is a popular tool among individual creators and marketing teams, so it's easy to see why the platform has placed less emphasis on learning design features and assessments.
I think the voice in my test video above is slightly robotic. The intonation in some parts of the script sounds off, such as the opening "Hello," which is a shame as visually I think my test came out pretty well.
I experienced failed video render issues during my testing. I was wondering if this is just me, but based on the complaints I've seen on Reddit, this appears to be a common issue with the platform. I was obviously able to get my video rendered, but aside from wasting my time, these failed renders also cost me credits.
During my testing I also spotted a few subtle lip texture artifacts (I had to look closely), which somewhat reduced overall avatar realism.
How does HeyGen compare to Colossyan?
If you are an instructional designer or L&D professional and care most about branching, assessments, and packaging content as training programs, then Colossyan is most likely the best option for you, as the platform features are specifically built with L&D and enablement teams in mind.
HeyGen can work for training videos, but it's really more of a general-purpose avatar platform that has only recently started adding some LMS-oriented features, such as support for SCORM exports.
HeyGen has much higher quality avatars than Colossyan does, so if avatar quality is your most important factor, then you should definitely go with HeyGen over Colossyan - it's the better fit for creators, marketing teams, and agencies.
3. Camtasia
URL:
https://www.techsmith.com/camtasia/
Quick summary
Avatar realism:
Medium realism
Lip-sync accuracy:
Medium accuracy
Voice quality:
High
Best use case:
Screen recording videos
Biggest limitation:
Weak avatar expressiveness and no branching scenarios
My experience
Pros
Camtasia is known for providing high-quality
screen recording software
, but now they offer avatars too. The avatar functionality relies on a script-based editor which is easy to use.
Looking at my test avatar video above, I think the output looks very clean and professional.
I think the voice quality is pretty good too. It looks like they use ElevenLabs voices, which explains why. The intonation sounds natural and the overall delivery is high quality.
Camtasia has some limited support for adding interactivity to your videos. It allows you to add quizzes, clickable hotspots, and interactive navigation features.
Camtasia supports
SCORM exports
.
Cons
‍Camtasia’s avatars almost completely lack expressiveness compared to the other, avatar-first platforms in this list. I’d compare it to Colossyan in that the avatars look almost lifeless. There are no visible hand gestures or full-body movement, and I think the lack of this really harms the realism.
The avatar library is quite small and offers a limited variety, and there’s no camera, pose control, or variation options, which are pretty much standard on avatar-first platforms.
Camtasia is probably not a good option if you’re looking to make avatar videos in multiple languages. The avatar functionality only supports 7 languages, and there’s also no support for different dialects or accent variations.
You’ll also need to pay for the higher-tier Camtasia plans (Camtasia Pro) to get access to the full AI avatar functionality.
Camtasia's support for interactivity doesn't extend to adding branching scenarios to your videos.
How does Camtasia compare to Colossyan?
I think that both Camtasia's and Colossyan's avatars suffer from a lack of expressiveness and realism, and I think both could be distracting to learners in the context of an avatar-led training video.
Although Camtasia now offers avatars via Camtasia Pro/Audiate, it's still primarily a tool for creating professional screen recording videos with a timeline-based editor.
If you are often creating training videos that are screen-recording-heavy (e.g. software walkthroughs, SOPs, or product tutorials) and you want maximum editing polish, Camtasia is the better fit. It gives you higher-quality screen capture with a timeline-based editor and optional AI avatars, although the stock avatar library is small and localization support is limited to only 7 languages.
Camtasia also supports interactivity including quizzes and clickable hotspots, but that doesn't include branching scenarios.
Colossyan is the better choice if you want an L&D-first and avatar-focused platform. It has its own screen recording tool (although it's not as advanced as Camtasia's), it also supports more than 100 languages, and it lets you add branching scenarios to your videos (as well as quizzes and clickable buttons).
4. Vyond
URL:
https://www.vyond.com/
Quick summary
Avatar realism:
Low realism
Lip-sync accuracy:
Low accuracy
Voice quality:
Low
Best use case:
Animated training videos
Biggest limitation:
Low avatar realism and no interactivity
My experience
Pros
I wasn’t very impressed with Vyond’s avatars, but one positive I can highlight is that Vyond offers a large library of avatars with over 1,100 to choose from and there's support for more than 70 languages.
I think Vyond's AI avatar functionality is best thought of as an add-on that lets L&D teams create simple talking presenter segments in their videos.
Vyond is best-known for being a platform that helps you to create engaging animated videos without any animation chops. I think that animated training video content can be a really good fit for some training video formats, such as
microlearning
and
scenario-based content
.
The platform works as a drag-and-drop editor, and makes it easy to start assembling your animated video and creating character-driven scenarios. That’s where I see Vyond’s biggest strength - it’s a tool that was made for storytelling.
The structured templates and flexible scene editing made my module look really good without being a Vyond expert.
Vyond supports SCORM exports to your LMS.
Cons
‍
I think Vyond’s avatars are the least realistic of all of the tools I mention in this list. To me the uncanny valley effect is pretty noticeable, and the avatar’s gestures and movements look super artificial. The facial expressions lack any emotional depth.
The lip sync isn’t great either. It seemed to get noticeably less precise when I tested longer sentences.
I don’t think the voice quality is very good. There are a number of unnatural pauses, and in general the voice sounds super robotic.
Aside from the avatars, I’d point out that animation might not always be the best option for your training video project. Despite the animated video quality itself being great, I think it could look slightly unprofessional in certain scenarios.
My problem with Vyond's animated videos is the production speed. It typically takes me more than 90 minutes to get to a usable first draft. All of the staging is manual (i.e. you have to build and arrange each scene yourself, rather than being able to rely on any form of automation).
There's also no support for brand kits, so getting your video to match your company's brand colors also takes quite a long time.
How does Vyond compare to Colossyan?
I'm not a fan of Vyond's or Colossyan's AI avatars. I think they both lack realism and will distract learners. If avatar realism is important to you I would look elsewhere.
If you don't use Vyond's avatars and stick with their animated style then Vyond can absolutely make sense in some scenarios. I think Vyond can be a great fit when you want to create narrative-driven animated training.
I would not recommend Vyond when you need fast production, rapid updates, or if you are covering topics where animation doesn’t work, such as technical training, or other more serious topics.
Vyond doesn't support creating interactive videos - you'd need to export your Vyond video to an authoring tool like Articulate Storyline or Adobe Captivate and add the interactivity there. If adding interactivity is important to you then Colossyan is a much better option than Vyond.
5. Elai
URL:
https://elai.io/
Quick summary
Avatar realism:
Medium/low realism
Lip-sync accuracy:
Medium accuracy
Voice quality:
Average
Best use case:
Avatar-led training videos
Biggest limitation:
Robotic AI voices
My experience
Pros
The first thing I noticed while testing out Elai was that the video rendering speed is extremely fast. I generated the above video in only 1 minute and 34 seconds, so it's definitely one of the fastest AI video tools I've ever used.
The Elai platform offers a range of document-to-video automation flows that give you plenty of options for converting existing content into avatar-driven videos.
I tried the workflows for
URL-to-video
and
PowerPoint-to-video
and they did a pretty good job of preserving the key points of my original content, although the videos generated would definitely need a bunch of editing.
Like Synthesia and Colossyan, Elai also offers a screen recording tool, a number of interactive learning features (including clickable links, branching logic, and Q&A sessions), and SCORM export support, so I think it definitely hits the minimum requirements for the L&D and training use case.
Cons
Looking at my test video, I think it's clear that Elai's avatar realism and expressiveness aren't that great. I can see video compositing issues around the avatar's hair which make the avatar look somewhat 'cut out' from the background.
The quality of the AI voices is also underwhelming. I found the delivery in my test video to be emotionally flat, and the delivery was even more robotic in the Spanish version of my test video.
Elai doesn't offer much in terms of B-roll options, since the platform doesn't have any integrated AI video generation models like Sora or Veo.
During my testing I experienced a bug when trying to generate my Spanish test video. The video generation kept failing despite the fact that I definitely had enough credits left. I'm not sure why this happened, but it seems like there are some video generation reliability issues.
How does Elai compare to Colossyan?
Elai and Colossyan are very similar on paper, since both are AI avatar platforms that target the L&D use case. Both offer the core functionalities that you need to create training videos like interactive video elements (quizzes and branching-style flows), SCORM export, and multi-language support.
They also both suffer from low-realism AI avatars, which makes me think that if you want to create engaging avatar-driven training videos it's better to use a different platform on this list.

Frequently asked questions
What is the best Colossyan alternative for avatar realism?
Synthesia is a great alternative to Colossyan as it offers all of the same L&D-friendly features that Colossyan does (interactivity, SCORM exports, multilingual support) while its AI avatars are much more realistic. You can pick an avatar from the stock library or create your own custom avatar in your likeness. Synthesia also lets you direct your avatars to take actions and supports AI-generated B-roll and motion graphics.
What is the best Colossyan alternative for screen-recorded training content?
All of the options on this list (Synthesia, HeyGen, Vyond, Elai) provide screen recording tools, but Camtasia is probably the go-to screen recording tool for L&D use cases like software training walkthroughs, SOPs, and product tutorials. If screen recording quality matters more than avatar realism and expressiveness, then Camtasia is a good option. If you want realistic avatars then Camtasia won't be a good fit.
What is the best free Colossyan alternative?
All of the tools on this list offer a free plan or free trial. You can find out about Synthesia's free plan on our
pricing page
.
What is the best Colossyan alternative for animated training videos?
If you want animated training videos instead of avatar-led training videos, then Vyond is worth a try. Animated videos can work well with narrative-driven or scenario-based training content. I wouldn't recommend using Vyond's AI avatars.
