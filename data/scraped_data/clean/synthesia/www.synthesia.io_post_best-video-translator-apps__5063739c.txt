What are the different ways to translate a video?
When
translating a video
, you have two options:
Subtitles
: Translating spoken content into written text displayed on-screen. Adding subtitles to videos allow viewers to read the translation while hearing the original audio, which keeps the original voices and emotions intact.
Dubbing
: Translating and replacing the original audio with a new track in the target language. This can be standard
voiceover dubbing
, where the video stays the same, or
AI lip-sync dubbing
, where the video is altered so the speaker’s mouth movements match the translated speech.
How I test AI video translation software

🈯️ What makes a great AI video translator app?

Accuracy
– How accurate are the subtitles generated? How accurate is the dubbed speech? Does the translation sound natural to a native speaker?

Voice cloning
– Does the dubbed voice sound like the original person, or is it a robotic-sounding AI voice?

Lip-sync
– Does the video visually adjust to match the new dubbed voice?

Editing features
– AI translations aren’t always perfect. You need to be able to edit subtitles, scripts, and audio to fix mistakes.

Language support
– How many languages can the tool translate into?

Ease of use
– Is the interface intuitive and easy to learn?

Pricing
– Is the service good value for money?

2.‎‎ AI Studios
What I liked
Generous free plan that lets you dub videos up to 3 minutes long
Offers both AI dubbing with lip-sync or voiceover dubbing
The most affordable rate for lip-synced AI dubbing (of acceptable quality) on this list
Accurate translations
Supports 150+ languages
What I didn't like
Quality issues with lip-sync not working and moments of jittering
What happened when I tried it
I really liked using AI Studios.
They let you do AI dubbing with lip-sync on videos up to 3 minutes long with a free account, which is the most generous of any tools on this list.
The translation is accurate, and in general the lip-sync quality is reasonable. I think the voice cloning works pretty well too - it sounds more or less like me.
AI Studios supports dubbing into 150 languages, so it's highly likely that they offer any language that you're after.
I played around a bit with the post-dubbing editing features and they were pretty solid too. You can refine the dubbed script to improve the timing, flow and accuracy which is great for avoiding additional generations and burning through all your credits.
The main issue I ran into with AI Studios was that the lip-sync drifted and there was some visible jittering in a few moments (you can spot it near the end of the video above). It’s the kind of thing I wouldn’t want in any video that really matters, especially in a professional setting.
However, if you're just using the free version for a quick translation of a video, then this might not matter much.
Pricing
AI Studios allows up to 3 minutes of AI dubbing a month on the Free plan. If you need more, you can buy the Personal plan for $29/month and get 120 minutes of dubbing allowance at a rate of $0.24/minute
How does it compare to the other options?
AI Studios offers AI dubbing with lip-sync of intermediate quality, but where it excels is its affordability.
It has the most generous free allowance for AI dubbing (with lip-sync) of all the tools in the list, and it also has the lowest cost per minute for additional AI dubbing minutes.
Who should use it?
I think AI Studios is a great option for those looking for quick AI video translation with lip-sync on a budget. The quality isn't to a super-professional standard, but if that doesn't matter for your use case, then I don't see why you use any other tool.
3. Perso AI
What I liked
Affordable lip-synced AI dubbing
Decent voice cloning
What I didn't like
You can't download your translated video on the Free plan
What happened when I tried it
Perso AI's lip-sync quality is pretty good, but it’s not at the level where I’d fully trust it for a high-stakes, polished business video. You can spot small timing issues and subtle mismatches, which are fine for quick demos or social content, but a bit distracting if you’re aiming for a very professional result.
The voice cloning is solid though. In my test, the cloned voice sounded close enough to my real voice that it didn’t feel like a generic AI narrator.
Perso AI also supports more than 32 languages, which covers most common business and content use cases.
One limitation to be aware of is the free plan. You can generate the dubbed video, but you can’t actually download it unless you upgrade, which makes the free tier more of a preview than something you can use end-to-end.
On the editing side, Perso AI does let you go back and tweak the translated script and timing, which is useful if the automatic translation isn’t quite right. You can correct wording, adjust pacing, and regenerate sections without starting from scratch.
Pricing
Perso AI's free plan lets you dub 1 minute of video, but it doesn't let you export your video. You can download your videos on the Creator plan though ($6.99/month)
However, the Creator plan gives you 15 mins of dubbing with lip-sync, at cost of $0.47/minute.
How does it compare to the other options?
I think Perso AI is another good option, but I feel like if you're looking for AI dubbing with lip-sync, you'll probably go with Synthesia or AI Studios as those options have superior dubbing quality, and they also both let you download your translated video on the free plan.
Who should use it?
If you're on a budget but you want to use AI dubbing with lip-sync.
4.‎ ElevenLabs
What I liked
5 mins of automatic watermarked voiceover dubbing for free
Great quality voice cloning that sounds just like me
Dedicated 'Dubbing Studio' platform with lots of editing options
What I didn't like
Voiceover dubbing only, with no lip-sync options
Some weird intonations in the dubbed audio that sounds a bit weird
Only supports 29 languages
Learning curve is a bit steep
What happened when I tried it
ElevenLabs is best known for its
AI voice generation
, and their Dubbing Studio is focused purely on high-quality voiceover translation, without any lip-sync.
In my tests, the voice cloning did a great job of preserving my tone and emotion, so the translated audio still sounded like me rather than a generic AI voice.
That said, I did notice a few strange moments in the output.
In the example above, when I say “compruébalo” (“check it out”), the intonation sounds a bit off and not quite natural. It’s a small thing, but in a professional video it’s the kind of detail that would need fixing.
The good news is that ElevenLabs gives you very granular editing controls.
You can tweak timing, pronunciation, tone, and phrasing, which makes it possible to correct these issues, although I found that the interface is a bit more advanced and comes with a learning curve.
In terms of language coverage, ElevenLabs supports 29 languages, mainly focused on the major global markets.
Pricing
The platform gives you 5 mins of automatic watermarked voiceover dubbing for free.
You can get access to ElevenLab's Dubbing Studio on the $5/month creator plan. That gets you 3 minutes per month of voiceover-only dubbing at a rate of $1.67/min, with additional minutes available for $1.50/min.
How does it compare to the other options?
The main difference between ElevenLabs and the other options on this list is that ElevenLabs offers no lip-sync.
However, the voice cloning is, as you might expect, one of the best of the tools listed here.
Who should use it?
I think ElevenLab's dubbing studio makes sense for creators who don't need lip-sync and are happy with voiceover dubbing only.
5.‎‎ Maestra
What I liked
Accurate translation with natural sounding Spanish
Cloned voice sounded just like me
Decent language coverage (support for more than 125 languages)
What I didn't like
The platform is super confusing to use
No voice cloning on the free plan
Lip-sync only available on the $200/month business plan
Pricing structure only really works for large translation projects
What happened when I tried it
One thing that I liked about Maestra was the accuracy of the translation and how natural the Spanish sounded. The wording felt human and fluent, and in most cases it matched what I actually said.
I didn’t love that voice cloning isn’t available on the free plan, although once I had paid and got voice cloning working, it sounded pretty spot on.
It took me quite a while to work out how to generate my translated video with the cloned voice. The platform isn't very user-friendly and it was quite frustrating trying to work out how to do simple tasks.
I didn't get to try the lip-sync as it's only available on the $200/month Business plan, and I didn't want to pay that much.
Pricing
Standard voiceover dubbing without voice cloning costs $0.41 per minute on the Basic plan ($49 for 120 minutes). Voice cloning becomes available on the Premium plan at $0.99 per minute ($99 for 100 minutes), while lip-sync dubbing requires the Business plan and costs $1.00 per minute ($200 for 200 minutes).
How does it compare to the other options?
Like Murf and Speechify, the free plan is highly limited by the lack of voice cloning.
Maestra's dubbing with lip-sync is still quite affordable in $ per minute terms, although not as cheap as AI Studios or Perso AI's.
The pricing structure makes sense for someone who needs to translate a lot of minutes, but doesn't really work if you only want to translate a quick video.
Who should use it?
Someone on a budget who has a large translation project.
6.‎ Kapwing
What I liked
Affordable pricing
Good voice cloning
Lots of useful editing features
What I didn't like
Lip-sync quality isn't great
Annoying restriction on dubbing short videos (less than 10 seconds long)
Some quality issues with default export options
Slightly confusing interface
What happened when I tried it
Kapwing makes it easy to translate videos quickly with subtitles and basic dubbing, and overall it’s a pretty accessible tool.
The interface is a bit overwhelming at first though, mainly because there’s so much other video editing functionality packed into the product. It took me a moment to find the dubbing and translation features among everything else.
The translation itself was solid. Subtitles were accurate, and the voice cloning sounded surprisingly good for a general-purpose video editor. However, the default export quality wasn’t great, and I noticed some roughness in the final video.
The lip-sync quality itself doesn't look that great either. I don't think the timing and the movements look very convincing.
One small but annoying limitation was that my video had to be at least 10 seconds long to run the dubbing, so I had to artificially extend it just to test the feature. On the plus side, Kapwing gives you a lot of post-editing control, so you can fine-tune subtitles, timing, and audio once the translation is done.
Kapwing supports dubbing into more than 40 languages.
Pricing
On Kapwing’s Pro plan, which costs $24 per month, you get up to 50 minutes of standard dubbing, working out to $0.48 per minute. Lip-sync dubbing is priced as an add-on on the same plan, with up to 30 minutes available, which equates to roughly $0.80 per minute on top of the base dubbing cost. When you combine the two, full lip-synced dubbing comes out at approximately $1.28 per minute.
How does it compare to the other options?
I think there are better options on this list than Kapwing. If I want lip-sync I'd prefer to use Synthesia, AI Studios, or Perso. If I just want voiceover dubbing I'd probably prefer to go with ElevenLabs.
I think it's quite similar to Veed (which is further down the list) in that it's a video editing platform with some dubbing functionality added on, but it's not a very good standalone dubbing solution.
Who should use it?
Users who want a video translation solution that comes with extensive video editing features.
7.‎ ‎Speechify
What I liked
Supports dubbing into 60+ languages
What I didn't like
No lip-sync (or at least I couldn't find it anywhere)
No voice cloning on the free plan
The translation wasn't quite right and changed the meaning of what I said
The platform was a bit buggy and got stuck a few times
It seems overpriced for what you get
What happened when I tried it
Speechify seems to be a voiceover dubbing-only tool.
First I tried the Free plan (with no voice cloning). The first thing I noticed is that the translation wasn’t quite accurate. It came out as “Echa un vistazo y verás cómo funciona,” but what I actually said was “Check it out,” which should be closer to “Échale un vistazo.” That’s how most of the other tools translated it too.
It might seem like a small difference, but the added “verás cómo funciona” changes the meaning to “and you’ll see how it works.” In a high-stakes or client-facing translation, these kinds of unprompted additions can be risky and could cause real problems.
I bought the Studio Starter plan to test out the voice cloning. Speechify is a bit different in that you have to clone your voice separately and then select that cloned voice when you translate your video, which is a bit of a nuisance - pretty much every other tool on this list just clones your voice automatically.
When I did get the voice cloning working, it sounded pretty weird as you can see in the video above. I don't pronounce "todos" in "Hola a todos" correctly, and I pronounce "doblar" (to dub) in a strange way that sounds like I'm a pirate.
Pricing
Speechify gives you 3 minutes and 20 seconds of voiceover dubbing on their Free plan. Beyond that you'll need their Studio Starter plan which costs $19/month and gives you 40 minutes of voiceover dubbing at a rate of $0.48/minute (and access to voice cloning).
Their documentation mentions that lip-sync is available, but I can't find it anywhere on their platform or on their pricing page.
How does it compare to the other options?
The Speechify platform looks and feels very similar to Perso AI's platform, but aside from that there aren't many similarities.
Since I couldn't find any details about any lip-sync functionality, it feels like Speechify is closer to Murf or ElevenLabs in that it only appears to offer voiceover dubbing.
It's kind of similar to Murf in that it doesn't offer voice cloning on the free plan too.
Who should use it?
I'm not really sure. I'd probably prefer to use ElevenLabs instead of Speechify.
8.‎ Murf
What I liked
100 minutes of voiceover dubbing on the free plan
What I didn't like
You can't download your translated video on the Free plan
No lip-sync available, voiceover dubbing only
Limited language options
Voice cloning only available on the Enterprise plan
What happened when I tried it
I was disappointed with Murf's dubbing solution.
Firstly, it's voiceover dubbing only, although you could argue that's fair enough since Murf is an AI voice-focused company.
While testing, I saw that the available language variations are super limited. For example, for Spanish they only offer Spanish (European) and Spanish (Mexico), and that's it.
While I was trying to download my translated video, I found that I couldn't export it as an MP4 (or any other format) without signing up for a paid plan. I understand these platforms have to make money but I found that a super annoying way to offer a free plan.
Lastly, I found that voice cloning functionality is only offered on the enterprise plan. It's understandable if the platform is largely geared at enterprise, but it really does make the tool less useful for the individual creator. I was instead stuck with one of the stock AI voices that you can hear on my video above. I think you'll agree it doesn't sound that great.
The one saving grace of Murf is that their Free plan allowance of 100 minutes of voiceover dubbing (without voice cloning) is still quite generous.
Murf supports dubbing into 40+ languages.
Pricing
Murf offers 100 minutes of voiceover dubbing to users on the Free plan. If you want additional minutes (and if you want to be able to export your videos) you'll need to pay a cost $0.5/minute in a pay as you go structure. Voice cloning is only available to enterprise users.
How does it compare to the other options?
Murf is similar to ElevenLabs in that it's primarily an AI voice generation platform that is offering a dubbing tool. As a result, there's no lip-sync functionality and it's entirely focused on voiceover dubbing.
The difference between Murf and ElevenLabs is that Murf really doesn't seem geared towards creators (whereas ElevenLabs absolutely is). The fact that Murf's voice cloning is only available to Enterprise plans is a big issue for me.
Who should use it?
If you need free voiceover dubbing minutes and don't care about voice cloning.
9.‎ ‎Veed
What I liked
Accurate translation
Veed has a nice interface with lots of other editing tools that might be useful
Dubbing into 125+ languages
What I didn't like
No freemium dubbing option
No freemium subtitles option
Lip-sync feels a bit off
Voice cloning didn't really work
What happened when I tried it
Although I was trying to test the freemium version of all of these tools, Veed doesn't offer any AI video translation functionality on their free plan, so I was forced to pay up just to even try it.
Veed has a nice interface and is easy to use. It's a potential advantage in that you can translate and then edit your video (e.g. add animations or transitions) all in one platform, but I personally didn't need any of that functionality.
There's an option to upload your own transcript too.
I particularly liked how the dubbed audio pronounced "Synthesia" in the way a natural Spanish speaker would. I selected the 'Match Video Voice' option but I don't think that the cloned voice sounds like me speaking Spanish.
Looking at the video again, I think the lip-sync is a bit off. If the tool was free I'd let that slide, but given that they made me pay I think that's not good enough.
Pricing
To access subtitle and AI dubbing video translation in Veed you'll need the Pro plan, which costs $55/month. The plan gives you 20 minutes of dubbing per month so that works out at a cost of $2.75/minute.
How does it compare to the other options?
I don't really see any benefits of using Veed's AI video translation tools. There's no free option, and the output isn't as good as some of the free options on this list.
Who should use it?
I don't really recommend Veed for AI video translation, but maybe it makes sense to use Veed if you want to do a bunch of editing to your video as well.
10.‎ ‎Rask AI
What I liked
Voice cloning quality wasn't bad
What I didn't like
You can't download your translated video on the Free plan
Very high price for voiceover dubbing ($2.40/minute)
Lip-sync quality is absolutely terrible
What happened when I tried it
Rask AI ended up being my least favourite tool on this list. The voice cloning quality itself wasn’t bad, and in isolation the generated voice sounded fairly natural.
On paper, it also looks strong with support for 135 languages, which is one of the widest language ranges I tested.
In practice though, the experience was frustrating.
You can’t download your translated video on the free plan. On top of that, the pricing for voiceover dubbing only is very high at around $2.40 per minute, which is similar to the cost of full dubbing with lip-sync on other platforms.
Rask's lip-sync is where things really fall apart. To even access it, you need the Creator Pro plan, which is extremely pricey. And from the previews I tested, the lip-sync quality was genuinely awful, with obvious mismatches and unnatural mouth movements. It was bad enough that I didn’t even bother paying just to export the result. I didn’t want to waste money on something I already knew I wouldn’t use.
Pricing
Rask AI's Creator plan offers 25 minutes of voiceover dubbing for $60/month (or $2.40). That cost per minute goes down a bit if you go for the $150/month Creator Pro plan (to $1.5/minute) which also gives you access to lip-sync.
How does it compare to the other options?
Badly. The cost of voiceover dubbing is equivalent to the cost of full AI dubbing with lip-sync on the other platforms in this list, yet Rask AI's lip-sync functionality appears to be almost broken.
Who should use it?
I don't recommend it for anybody.

What is the best AI video translation tool?
The best AI video translation tool depends on your specific needs, but Synthesia stands out for enterprise use with its high-quality lip-sync, voice cloning, and professional workflows. For businesses needing accurate translations that maintain brand consistency across global markets, Synthesia offers the most comprehensive solution with support for 139+ languages and secure editing features that protect meaning and regulatory language.
If you're looking for budget-friendly options, AI Studios provides excellent value with up to 3 minutes of free lip-synced dubbing and the lowest per-minute rates for paid plans. For content creators and small teams who need quick translations without breaking the bank, AI Studios delivers acceptable quality at just $0.24 per minute, making it ideal for social media content and informal communications where perfect lip-sync isn't critical.
How do I translate a video with AI using lip-sync and voice cloning?
To translate a video with AI lip-sync and voice cloning, you simply upload your video file to a platform like Synthesia, select your target language, and the AI automatically clones your voice and adjusts the mouth movements to match the translated speech. The process typically takes just a few minutes: upload your video in MP4 or MOV format, choose from available languages, review the translated transcript for accuracy, and download your dubbed video with perfectly synchronized lip movements that look natural in the new language.
The technology works by analyzing your original voice patterns to create a synthetic version that sounds like you speaking the target language, while simultaneously adjusting the video frames to match the new phonemes and timing. Most modern AI video translation tools also let you edit the translated script before finalizing, ensuring technical terms, brand names, and important phrases are translated correctly for your specific audience and use case.
Can I translate a YouTube video with AI?
Yes, you can translate YouTube videos with AI using tools like Synthesia that offer YouTube video translation features. Simply paste the YouTube URL into the translation tool, select your target language, and the AI will download the video, transcribe the audio, translate the content, and create a dubbed version with voice cloning and lip-sync in your chosen language.
The process preserves the original speaker's voice characteristics while adapting the mouth movements to match the new language, creating a natural viewing experience for international audiences. This capability is particularly valuable for businesses wanting to repurpose existing YouTube content for global markets, educators sharing knowledge across language barriers, and content creators looking to expand their reach without re-recording videos from scratch.
Is there a free AI video translator I can try?
Several AI video translation tools offer free plans that let you test their capabilities before committing to a paid subscription. Synthesia provides free translation for videos up to 1 minute with full lip-sync and voice cloning features, while AI Studios offers the most generous free tier with up to 3 minutes of dubbed video per month, making it ideal for trying out AI translation on longer content.
When evaluating free options, pay attention to whether you can actually download your translated video, as some tools like Perso AI and Rask AI generate the translation but require a paid plan to export the final file. For the best free experience that includes both high-quality output and the ability to download your video, Synthesia and AI Studios provide the most complete solutions, letting you experience professional-grade AI video translation without upfront investment.
How does Synthesia compare to other video translation software for enterprise needs?
Synthesia is specifically designed for enterprise video translation with features that other tools lack, including secure translation editing, API support for bulk dubbing, and governance controls that match corporate risk profiles. Unlike consumer-focused tools, Synthesia provides SSO-protected video pages, workspace-level permissions, brand kit enforcement, and pronunciation controls that make it safe to deploy across large organizations with strict compliance requirements.
The platform excels at handling entire content libraries rather than one-off videos, with Excel add-ins and template systems that enable systematic translation of training materials, product demos, and corporate communications at scale. While tools like Kapwing or Veed might work for occasional translations, Synthesia's combination of high-quality lip-sync, professional support, SCORM compatibility, and integration with existing L&D workflows makes it the clear choice for businesses that need reliable, consistent video localization as part of their global communication strategy.
