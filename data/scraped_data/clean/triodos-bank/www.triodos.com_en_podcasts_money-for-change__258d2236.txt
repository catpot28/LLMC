Podcast Money for Change
In our Money for Change podcast, the Impact & Economics team at Triodos Bank dives into the latest developments in sustainability transitions. Every week, one of our hosts (Emma, Sanne and Ernst) discusses a question with Chief Economist Hans Stegeman and an expert on the topic. We aim to bring you the latest insights on what it takes to create a more balanced and just world, and what the role of money is in getting us there.
Feel free to share any thoughts, suggestions, ideas or feedback on our podcast by using the form at the bottom of this page.

Episode Overview | July – September 2025
July 9 | Who is funding the future of the Central Arctic Ocean, and can money help protect it?
In this episode, we talk about the current state of the Arctic, a region which is known for its vital function within the Earth’s ecosystem, its unique wildlife and is inhabited by various indigenous communities. However, economic exploitation has put the Arctic under pressure. These activities create severe environmental risks, which makes it necessary to take measures that safeguard the Arctic Ocean and its surrounding ice caps. One such initiative is the Arctic Oceans Pledge, a voluntary commitment by companies to avoid using the sea-ice passageways in their global supply chains. Joining us is Alex Shahbazi, Senior Manager Artic Programs at Ocean Conservancy, to discuss the role of money in the protection of the Central Arctic Ocean.
This episode is recorded in English​​

July 3 | The biggest contributors to polution are spared; how do we create an industry within ecological limits?
In this episode, we discuss the Dutch CO2 tax. The CO2 tax for large industrial polluters such as Shell, Tata Steel and ASML was hailed as one of the means to achieve our country’s national climate goals. After years of delay, collection of the tax was finally started this year, but before collection could really start, the tax was already abolished by a majority in the Lower House. So, in this podcast, we discuss how we can create an alternative industrial policy that does fit within the ecological boundaries of the Netherlands. What does such a transition look like? And how do we ensure that workers who lose their jobs are properly supported in finding new work?
This episode is recorded in Dutch​​

Episode Overview | April – June 2025
June 25 | Profit from injustice; what role did the financial sector play in slavery's past?
In this episode, we look forward to Keti Koti. On July 1st, we celebrate the abolition of slavery in the Netherlands. Hundreds of years ago, Dutch trading companies like the VOC and WIC facilitated the slave trade. But they were not solely responsible for the atrocities of the time; the financial sector was intimately involved in this part of history. Bankers and investors profited from trading enslaved people, thus functioning as catalysts for the system. Together with Erik Odegard, researcher at the International Institute of Social History, we examine the role of financial institutions in the history of slavery. We look at the dynamics of that time and draw parallels to the current system. What lessons for transitions can we draw from this? We also discuss the abolition of slavery; how was this change initiated and what role did the financial sector play in it?
This episode is recorded in Dutch​​

June 18 | How do we transform economics for sustainability?
In this episode, we examine the central question in Hans’ PhD research: why do current economic models consider economics to be separate from social and ecological domains? Are they not intertwined in the real world? Yes, they are, is the answer. Then why do economists still work with welfare models that do not include natural and social capital? Why aren't these assumptions more actively questioned? And what can we do at the policy level to ensure that we build an economic system that stays within the boundaries of all three domains? Along with Anna and Ernst, Hans addresses these fundamental questions. An episode that may change your view of economics forever.
This episode is recorded in Dutch​​

May 21 | Can financing nature help protect the wild places on earth, or are we chasing a fantasy by commodifying it?
In this episode, we talk about nature-based solutions. These are initiatives aimed at restoring, enhancing or protecting ecosystems by changing the intended use of land. For example, reforestation projects on land that was previously used for agricultural purposes can create positive outcomes for the biodiversity and the storage of carbon. While the environmental benefits are clear, creating viable revenue models for these public goods remains a major challenge. Together with our colleague and Nature-based Solutions expert Simon Scholl, we’ll tackle a complex question: can financing nature help protect the wild places on earth, or are we chasing a fantasy by commodifying it?

May 13 | How do we solve the housing crisis?
In this episode, we talk about houses. The lack of them, that is. The current housing shortage in the Netherlands exists because of political choices in the past. Consider, for example, the 11 billion euro subsidy for homeowners, most of which goes to the very wealthiest, in the form of mortgage interest deductions or the non-taxation of home excess value. These (indirect) subsidies only widen the gap between homeowners and non-homeowners in wealth. Together with Cody Hochstenbach, urban geographer and researcher at the University of Amsterdam, we analyze the causes and potential solutions of the current housing shortage.
This episode is recorded in Dutch​​

May 7 | Dutch Overshoot Day: What is it and how do we get rid of it?
In this episode, we zoom in on “Dutch Overshoot Day”. This is the day when, if the whole world lived like the Dutch do, we would have used up the total amount of renewable natural resources in the world. In 2025, Dutch Overshoot Day took place on May 5th. Since that day, we Dutch have effectively been depleting the earth's natural resources, which we then call “overshoot”. Together with Mark van Oorschot, a scientific researcher at the Netherlands Environmental Assessment Agency and an expert on biodiversity, we paint a picture of both our demand for these natural resources and the actual available biocapacity.

March 12 | The EU Omnibus: Why weakening rules for businesses is a disaster for a future-proof economy
In this episode, we discuss the EU Omnibus. This is a proposal designed by the European Commission to bundle and simplify a number of laws aimed at sustainability and corporate social responsibility. One of the directives that is part of this collection is the so-called “Corporate sustainability due diligence directive" (CSDDD). This directive requires companies to take action when risks of violating human rights and environmental laws occur. Our guest in this episode, Lara Wolters, is one of the instigators and creators of this directive and is part of the European Parliament on behalf of GroenLinks-PvdA.

In this episode, we talk about what’s known as social tipping points. Together with professor of socio-economic transitions Derk Loorbach, we explore what proportion of society is needed to achieve our ‘social tipping points’ and how the institutional resistance that we see in politics amongst others can be interpreted as a sign of us actually coming closer to these tipping points.
